ℹ️ About Microsoft DNSMOS Speech Quality

DNSMOS (estimated) is a deep learning-based, non-intrusive (reference-free) speech quality evaluation metric developed by Microsoft Research for the Deep Noise Suppression (DNS) Challenge. It evaluates audio as human raters do across three ITU-T P.835 perceptual dimensions:

  • SIG (Speech Signal Quality): Measures naturalness, presence of distortions, robotic artifacts, and speech clipping (1.0 = very unnatural/distorted, 5.0 = highly natural).
  • BAK (Background Noise Intrusiveness): Measures noticeability and intrusiveness of background noise (1.0 = very intrusive noise, 5.0 = completely quiet/imperceptible noise).
  • OVRL (Overall Quality): Composite human listening Mean Opinion Score (1.0 = poor, 5.0 = excellent).
Telephony & Bandwidth Considerations: DNSMOS models are trained on 16 kHz wideband audio. Narrowband telephony audio (e.g. G.711 µ-law/A-law, AMR-NB, G.729 at 8 kHz) is bandlimited to 4 kHz. When upsampled to 16 kHz for evaluation, the absence of high-frequency harmonics can result in lower overall scores than wideband speech.
Independence from E-model MOS: DNSMOS is a standalone speech quality model that evaluates acoustic degradation, speech distortion, and background noise. It does not represent network loss or delay (which are modeled separately by the ITU-T G.107 E-model).
